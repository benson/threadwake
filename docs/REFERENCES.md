# Design references inspected

Design references inspected on 2026-10-04. Original posts are references, not licenses to reuse their art.

- [Thomas Ricouard, Evergrow art tooling](https://x.com/Dimillian/status/2097587442538738110): procedural drawing engine plus tools for browsing and refining game art. Video visibly showed a **Motion & equipment** workshop with equipment grids, held/inventory variants and category tabs. Applied here: game and workshop share authored sprite functions and animation parameters.
- [BartekDev, sprite stacking](https://x.com/BartekK349090/status/2095660530543309098): tutorial about sprite stacking. The post text was inspected; its entire video was not analyzed. Potential later route for pseudo-3D props, not a claim about Threadwake's current renderer.
- [Garrett Petersen, baked ship headings](https://x.com/GarrettPetersen/status/2095750463413268917): 32 headings under a fixed 45-degree camera; pre-baked shadows and pixel heights for sinking. Applied principle: keep view conventions fixed and art direction reproducible.
- [Chris Zeuch quoting ranbla's procedural Pokémon map-flow tools](https://x.com/chriszeuch/status/2106302929728594169): visible post. Supports separately inspectable world tools; no unobserved implementation details inferred.
- [Emil Kowalski, motion vocabulary](https://x.com/emilkowalski/status/2061426518333571576): shared terminology for precise animation iteration.
- [Anshu, visual target workflow](https://x.com/anshuc/status/2096008086901113339): concept targets followed by in-game screenshot iteration. Threadwake uses original authored pixel clusters and visual checks.

[Thariq's action editor](https://x.com/trq212/status/2105849295580889208). Its video shows action phases named anticipation, rise, impact, follow, settle and recover, frame stepping, curves, and before/after views. Threadwake exposes those phases as animation parameters and uses the same pose evaluator in the game and workshop.

Threadwake's temporal thread, captured-projectile garden and co-op weaving are original design work for this project. No claim of historical uniqueness has been established.
